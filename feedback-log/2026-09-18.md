### 2. Confirmed bug: phrase-side typed cues double-count into the word-side buckets

Reading the code resolves the other half of yesterday's question, and it is a real defect.

`PhraseDrillBlock` renders the *same* `Cloze` and `ProductionTyping` components the word drill uses
(`components/learn/PhraseDrillBlock.tsx:209,239`). Those children fire their own telemetry unconditionally —
there is no surface/`cueType` prop gating it:

- `ProductionTyping` → `production_correct` / `production_close_correct` / `production_wrong`
- `Cloze` → `cloze_correct` / `cloze_wrong`

Meanwhile the parent fires `phrase_drill_correct` / `phrase_drill_wrong` with `payload.cueType`. So **one phrase
production attempt emits two events**, and `CUE_FROM_EVENT` (`lib/db/pedagogy-queries.ts:28`) files them in two
different rows: `phrase_production` (from the parent) and `production` (from the child, whose `ELSE` branch just
strips the suffix).

That is exactly why the pairs tie: `production` 34 == `phrase_production` 34, `cloze` 21 == `phrase_cloze` 21.

Two consequences:
- The `production` and `cloze` rows on `/admin/pedagogy` are **not word-level measurements**. They are phrase
  attempts wearing a word-level label. The child even passes `wordId={phrase.id}` for production, so the payload
  id is a phrase id.
- The `drill` bucket is **absent from the digest entirely**, and `production` exactly equals `phrase_production`.
  Both say the same thing: `DrillBlock` (the word-level drill) fired **nothing at all in 30 days**. Every drill
  event on record came from the phrase side.

This is the failure mode `CLAUDE.md` warns about under Measurement — a row that reads as a real measurement but
isn't one.

### 3. The 0% on typed phrase cues is a difficulty finding, not (yet) a grader bug

With the double-count understood, the underlying data is: **55 typed phrase attempts, 0 passed; 28 MCQ
recognition attempts, 13 passed (46%).** The grader is not uniformly broken — recognition scores fine, and
`production_close_correct` has its own bucket which would catch near-misses. It is absent, so there were no
near-misses either.

The likely cause is the ask itself. Phrase production passes the **entire phrase** as the target
(`correctTarget={phrase.text_target}`, `PhraseDrillBlock.tsx:242`), graded by `fuzzyMatchAnswer` against
`allowedEditsFor`, which caps tolerance at **5 edits total** (`lib/pedagogy/normalize.ts:61-66` —
`Math.min(5, floor(len/4))`). That cap was designed for single words. Asking a learner to reproduce a whole
sentence from an English prompt, within 5 characters, unaided, is close to a pass rate of zero by construction —
which is what the 0/34 says.

Worth noting this is the same shape as the "learning stopped" feedback that produced the drill exit cap: a cue
nobody can pass keeps re-queueing.

# Learn it until you know it (30 Sep 2026)

Ben, 30 Sep: "In Duolingo each piece is isolated and I repeat it multiple times. Here I go through 20
pieces and there's no repetition. Until I remember the content, you shouldn't move on."

He was right, and the data showed why.

## What was wrong (his own data, 30 Sep)

- **Nothing came back after a scene.** A scene teaches ~12 words and 5 phrases and drills each one 2–3
  times in ten minutes. Then nothing, until he opened Review. The 11 Aug scene's words were never
  seen again before 29 Sep.
- **Review was a flip card he graded himself.** Tap, see the answer, press Easy. Nothing said or
  typed. On 29 Sep he tapped Easy 13 times, 3–5 seconds apart, and the scheduler sent all 13 to
  **365 days** — after São Paulo.
- **New scenes were never held back**, and the weakest items waited behind the best-known ones.
- **The path went café → market → bus before the family.** At a realistic pace he'd have arrived
  knowing bus Portuguese, not "Esta é a minha mãe".
- The can-do check had no "I don't know" (he typed English and got a strike), and a double submit
  showed a raw "403 Already certified" that looked like a usage limit.

## The loop now

1. **Learn a scene**, then **"Lock these in"**: a short round on exactly those words and phrases.
2. **Every card asks for an answer first.** Say it (the mic listens in Portuguese and shows what it
   heard), type it, or hear it and pick the meaning. "I don't know" is always there.
3. **The app grades the answer, not you.** No Easy button until after the trip. If the mic mishears
   you, "I said it right" counts it (as a harder answer).
4. **A miss comes back** a few cards later, from the other angle, until you get it. Three tries, then
   it waits for next time (no endless loops).
5. **Half a phrase counts as half.** It shows which words were missing, asks for just those, then
   the whole phrase again. Little words (a, de, o…) and names don't count against you.
6. **It comes back tomorrow**, then in ~3 days, ~a week, and so on — but nothing is scheduled past
   17 Dec, and the last week before the trip sweeps everything once.
7. **A new scene waits** while more than 8 recent items are shaky. It says "Lock in N first";
   "Start the scene anyway" is still there.
8. **Family first.** After the four scenes you've done: arriving at the family's house, who's who,
   talking about yourself, the Sunday churrasco, meeting the mother, Christmas dinner — then the rest.

## Why these numbers

- One correct recall after a gap is the evidence an item is "known" for today (successive-relearning
  research: relearning to 1 correct per session, then spaced sessions, beats drilling to 5 in one go).
- Graded attempts over self-ratings: learners overrate what they know, especially after a reveal.
- The trip cap: the best gap before a test is roughly 10–30% of the time until it; a third of the
  days left (between 7 and 30 days) keeps every trip word checked several times before 20 Dec.
- Only shaky items block new scenes, so a large pile of well-known words can't stop progress.

## Not done yet (worth doing next)

- A one-minute mic check on the first sitting, if "I said it right" gets used a lot.

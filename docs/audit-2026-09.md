# WordZoo — audit, 2026-09-16

Question this answers: **how far is WordZoo from "a stranger on a phone can try it, learn five
words that stick, see the real loop, and want to come back tomorrow"** — and what closes the gap.

Method: headless Chromium at 390×844 (and 1280×800) against the local dev server on the
production database, as a signed-out visitor, then as two fresh non-admin users
(`stranger@wordzoo.dev` on the legacy loop, `stranger2@wordzoo.dev` with the v2 slices forced on
by URL). Screenshots in `docs/audit-2026-09/before/` and `after/` — 65 of them, which stay on the machine because the repo ignores `*.png`. The dozen that carry the argument are committed in `docs/audit-2026-09/evidence/`, so this document's claims can be checked from GitHub; the file names cited in the tables below are from the full local set. Learner numbers
are straight SQL against Neon on 2026-09-16. The codebase survey was a full grep pass, not a sample.

### The same scene with v2 forced on (`?p2.restructure=1&p2.production=1&p2.cloze=1&p2.conversation=1&p2.distractors=1`)

Dialogue → phrase cards with a **word-by-word breakdown** ("Tap a word for its memory hook") →
"You've seen the breakdown. Now let's lock these phrases in." → a mixed drill: PICK THE PHRASE,
TYPE IT, FILL THE BLANK, with a "0 OF 5 LOCKED IN" strip and per-item dots. This is a real
product. It is also where two things go wrong:

1. **The typing drill's input sits at y≈590 of 844 on a phone** (`94-v2-4-btn_Continue_.png`).
   An iOS keyboard is ~300px tall, so the input and the Check button are under it.
   `useViewportInsets` is wired in `Cloze.tsx:70/285` and `TutorChat.tsx:159/477` but **not** in
   `ProductionTyping.tsx` — the component that carries every "type it" turn for words *and*
   phrases. This is the top real-feedback cluster ("when the keyboard opens on mobile it just
   pushes everything away").
2. **A learner who can't produce a phrase yet never leaves the drill.** My stranger pressed
   "I don't remember", was shown the answer ("Answer: Senang bertemu · type it to lock it in"),
   typed it, and was re-queued — **80 presentations, 0 of 5 locked in**. `leitner.ts` requires
   2 unaided cue types per item and `applyWrong` re-queues forever; a copied answer correctly
   counts as nothing. This is the mechanism behind the single most damning feedback on record
   ("We seem to have hit an era where the learning stopped" — 83 minutes on 4 phrases). The
   design is right that copying isn't knowing; it is wrong that there is no exit.

Everything else in v2 worked as a non-admin: progress persisted across reloads, XP fired, no
API errors, no console errors beyond the dev-only session refetch.

The digest's "183 words + 81 phrases overdue" is these three accounts summed. Nothing has been
reviewed since 2026-08-10. The founder's 128 overdue words are on average **111 days** late;
113 of them are graduated (interval 6–365 days), 15 are still in the 10-minute learning step
and have been for ~61 days.

**Retention by prior interval** (`srs_review_recorded`, 193 events, all time):

| Prior interval | n | Retained | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (learning step) | 112 | 82% | scene |
| 1d | 17 | 94% | scene |
| 4–7d | 63 | **56%** | scene |
| 15d+ | 1 | 100% | review |

Every event but one came from **in-scene drills**, not `/review`. So the "cliff from 94% at 4
days to 56% at 7" is what happens when a word met in a scene is re-met in a *later scene* a
week later, with no review in between. It is a true forgetting curve, and the review queue that
should have caught it was never opened. The scheduler itself is healthy (mean ease 2.52, 0
leeches, p95 interval 91d, nothing over the 365d cap).

**Drill accuracy** (`pedagogy_events`): production 54 right / 30 wrong / 2 close (63%),
cloze 31/15 (67%), phrase recognition+production 54/35 (61%), word recognition 66 right
(the wrong branch isn't emitted). The "13.6% phrase_production cue accuracy" quoted in the last
real digest comes from a different denominator (per-cue payloads) and can't be reproduced from
the raw rows; the per-drill numbers above are the honest ones.

**What the numbers imply.** The review queue is not a scheduling problem, it is an
*absence* problem: nobody reviewed for five weeks and every item aged past 8 days together.
Two things follow. (a) When the founder comes back, the SRS punishes him: an item with a
6-day interval that is 111 days late and answered *Good* gets `6 × 2.5 = 15` days — it ignores
that he just proved 111 days of retention. Anki credits the elapsed time; we don't. (b) With
185 items due and a 20-item cap per sitting, the queue empties in ~10 sittings *if* each
sitting drains 20 — but items answered Again come straight back in 10 minutes, so it doesn't.
Design in §6.

Content: 587 global mnemonics, all with image + audio. 40 Indonesian scenes, 161 phrases,
all with composite images and audio. 46 can-dos across 12 scenes. 201 `scene_pattern_exercises`
rows never rendered.

## 7. Overdue-queue design (before code)

Problem: 185 items all 8+ days late for one learner; a queue nobody can clear reads as
"the app is broken", not "I am behind".

Non-goals: no new SRS, no schema change, no silent rewriting of learner state.

1. **Late-review credit** (`schedule()` in `lib/srs/engine.ts`, review-source, graduated, correct only).
   Let `delay = max(0, daysSinceLastReview − interval)`. Then
   Hard: `(interval + delay/4) × 1.2`, Good: `(interval + delay/2) × ease`, Easy: `(interval + delay) × ease × 1.3`.
   With `delay = 0` this is byte-for-byte the current formula, so nothing changes for a learner who is on time. A 6-day item answered Good 111 days late goes to ≈ 150 days instead of 15. This is Anki's rule and the reason an Anki backlog drains.
2. **Retention-first ordering** (`getDueWordsForReview` / `getDuePhrasesForReview`): `ORDER BY (days overdue) / GREATEST(interval_days, 1) ASC, next_review_at ASC`. An item barely late relative to its interval is the one most likely still known; a returning learner meets their wins first and the Again-in-10-minutes churn happens at the end of the sitting, not the start.
3. **Honest framing**: the entry header already shows `1/5`; `ReviewComplete` already says "N more waiting". Add the backlog age to the review entry when the oldest due item is > 14 days ("N waiting since 26 Apr — 20 a sitting, most-likely-known first").

Not doing: an "amnesty" that resets or dismisses old items — that is a product decision about
the founder's own data, and (1)+(2) make the queue drain without it.
